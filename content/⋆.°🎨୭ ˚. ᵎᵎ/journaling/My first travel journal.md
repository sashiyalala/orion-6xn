Around the beginning of the year, I got invited into a group to do *travel journals*.
>[!definition] travel journal
> An internet phenomenon that came out of the journaling community consisting of a group of people setting up a journal and exchanging it with each other. 

The way we did it was for each of us to set up a journal and agree to send it over about once a month to the same person (always) in an established circle of contact.

The agreed plan was to get an A5 journal and I chose the Midori dotted grid. I've never particularly liked dotted paper. I chose it to get a *bit* out of my comfort zone.

I ended up deciding to paint something abstract on the cover. That's one of my favourite features of the Midori journals, they lend themselves perfectly to craftiness and creativity!

>[!info] Writing material
> Most of the text was written with my *Pilot Kaküno* with an $<EF>$ nib, inked with *De Atramentis Document Ink - Urban Grey*.
> Very sparsely, I added some minor details/titles with my *TWSBI Eco* with a $<Stub \,\, 1.1>$ nib, inked with *Kobe Ink - Hydrangea*.

# Character page
My main idea was to set up a *commonplace book*, and I knew that I wanted to take the opportunity to learn and make some entries about some of the instruments of the JWST. Again, I worried a bit because I didn't want to make it seem as if I wanted to make something strictly academic.
In order to try to convey that, I had also recommended to dedicate a spread to a character the book-bearer-for-the-month likes. Of course, I chose Pingu.

## About printing and collaging
I really like using my printer to put whatever images I want to collage in to my spreads. Additionally, I enjoy cutting them out with my scissors. 
It reminds me of when I was little. Due to the fact we didn't really have many toys, we entertained ourselves by making *collages* with magazines and newspapers that came in the mail or we gathered from a mall we frequented in our city. I vividly remember cutting out telephones from advertisement magazines, from maybe even before I had my first phone at all! And carefully cutting out the black screens to put flowers and other assorted images from other magazines. It may sound a bit sad, but I am really thankful for all the creativity muscles I flexed during that time, as well as the dexterity I developed with my hands. I feel like my love for creating with my hands comes from that time and those experiences.

# Commonplace entries
## JWST instruments
As I said, I decided to dedicate my spreads to the instruments of the JWST. I decided to learn about the most used ones in regards to photography's, so I chose at least a picture that was made for each of the instruments.
### Investigation and text preparation

First of all, I redacted my text. I did want to make it educational and accurate, so I took the task as proper investigation and I gathered notes from a few official sources, mostly NASA. I wrote everything down in a digital document and then I started working on the pages.
![[IMG_20260216_174850~2.jpg]]
Every source is cited in the pages.

### Layout planning
I was on a very minimal and clean streak, so I decided to add a bit of artsy decoration, in this case with highlighters, to add some color to the pages.
![[IMG_20260216_202336.jpg]]
I like using washi tape to frame the pages.
Before sticking the pictures I printed (with double-sided sticky tape), I like to map out the layout. In this case, I went page by page, to make sure I aligned each picture with the correct corresponding piece of text, i.e. with each instrument. I decided to use washi tape as section dividers as well.

# Set up (part 2) - Index

I like putting indexes in my books, specially the commonplace ones. Why do I record this information if not for future reference? I want to be able to access it easily!
I don't like putting indexes in the beginning because you don't know how many pages you will need before you end the book! Aaaaa
I learned this little...does it classify as a *trick*, even? A long time ago from...I don't remember.
It is to put at the end, but flipped vertically, so that you can easily access it by flipping it and bookmarking it (I put a washi tape tab, easy to access).
